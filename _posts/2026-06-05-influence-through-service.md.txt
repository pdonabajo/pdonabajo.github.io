# Influence Through Service

## Understanding Biblical Leadership as Stewardship

*"For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many."* — Mark 10:45

### Introduction

Leadership is one of the most sought-after pursuits in the world.

People desire influence.

They pursue recognition.

They seek positions of authority.

They aspire to lead organizations, communities, businesses, ministries, and nations.

Yet the Kingdom of God defines leadership differently from the world.

In many cultures, leadership is associated with power, status, prestige, and control.

In God's Kingdom, leadership is rooted in service, character, stewardship, and accountability.

The greatest leaders are not those who seek power for themselves.

The greatest leaders are those who faithfully serve God's purposes and God's people.

At The Frequency Live, we believe leadership is not about elevating oneself above others. Leadership is about using God-given influence to advance His Kingdom and serve others faithfully.

Leadership is not ownership.

Leadership is stewardship.

Every leader will one day stand before God and give an account for how they used their influence.

---

# Leadership Begins With Submission to God

Before a person can lead others effectively, they must first learn to follow God faithfully.

Every biblical leader was first a servant of God.

Moses followed God before leading Israel.

Joshua followed God before leading a nation.

David followed God before becoming king.

The apostles followed Christ before leading the Church.

Leadership in the Kingdom begins with surrender.

A leader who is not submitted to God's authority cannot properly exercise authority.

This is why the doctrine of **Final Authority** is essential to leadership.

A Christian leader does not ultimately answer to public opinion, personal ambition, or cultural expectations.

A Christian leader answers to God.

Psalm 75:6-7 reminds us:

> "For exaltation comes neither from the east nor from the west nor from the south. But God is the Judge: He puts down one, and exalts another."

Promotion comes from God.

Authority comes from God.

Leadership begins with submission to God.

---

# Character Before Charisma

One of the greatest mistakes in modern leadership is valuing charisma above character.

Charisma may attract people.

Character sustains influence.

A gifted communicator may gather a crowd.

A person of godly character earns trust.

The qualifications for leadership in Scripture focus primarily on character rather than talent.

Paul writes:

> "A bishop then must be blameless, the husband of one wife, temperate, sober-minded, of good behavior, hospitable, able to teach."
>
> 1 Timothy 3:2

Notice what Paul emphasizes:

* Integrity
* Self-control
* Faithfulness
* Maturity
* Good reputation

Leadership in God's Kingdom is not built upon personality alone.

It is built upon character.

Talent can open doors.

Character determines whether a leader remains effective once those doors open.

---

# Service Before Status

Jesus completely redefined leadership.

When His disciples argued about greatness, He responded:

> "Whoever desires to become great among you shall be your servant."
>
> Mark 10:43

The world often associates greatness with visibility.

Jesus associated greatness with service.

The Kingdom model of leadership is not climbing over others.

It is lifting others.

The Kingdom leader asks:

* How can I help?
* How can I serve?
* How can I strengthen others?
* How can I advance God's purposes?

Jesus demonstrated this principle perfectly.

Though He possessed all authority, He washed His disciples' feet.

Though He was Lord of all, He served all.

The greatest example of leadership is not found in a throne.

It is found at a cross.

---

# Integrity Before Influence

Influence without integrity is dangerous.

People often focus on how much influence they have rather than how they obtained it.

Biblical leadership teaches that influence must be built upon truth, honesty, and righteousness.

Proverbs 11:3 states:

> "The integrity of the upright will guide them."

Integrity means being the same person in private that you are in public.

It means faithfulness when nobody is watching.

It means honesty when dishonesty appears easier.

It means consistency between belief and behavior.

Many leaders have lost influence because they neglected integrity.

God is more concerned about who a leader becomes than how popular that leader becomes.

The foundation of lasting influence is integrity.

---

# Responsibility Before Recognition

Many people desire the rewards of leadership without embracing its responsibilities.

Scripture presents leadership as a burden of stewardship rather than a pathway to personal gain.

James writes:

> "Let not many of you become teachers, knowing that we shall receive a stricter judgment."
>
> James 3:1

Leadership carries responsibility.

Leaders influence people.

Leaders shape culture.

Leaders make decisions that affect others.

For this reason, leaders must recognize that influence comes with accountability.

Kingdom leaders do not pursue recognition.

They embrace responsibility.

Recognition may come.

Responsibility comes first.

---

# Leadership Is Stewardship

One of the most important principles of biblical leadership is stewardship.

A steward manages what belongs to another.

Everything a leader possesses ultimately belongs to God.

This includes:

* Influence
* Resources
* Opportunities
* Skills
* Positions
* Relationships

None of these are owned permanently.

They are entrusted temporarily.

Jesus taught:

> "Moreover it is required in stewards that one be found faithful."
>
> 1 Corinthians 4:2

Faithfulness is the primary requirement of stewardship.

God does not merely evaluate success.

God evaluates faithfulness.

The question is not simply:

*"How much influence did you have?"*

The question is:

*"What did you do with the influence I entrusted to you?"*

---

# Leadership Requires Wisdom

Leadership involves guiding people through challenges, decisions, and opportunities.

For this reason, leaders need wisdom.

Proverbs teaches:

> "Where there is no counsel, the people fall; but in the multitude of counselors there is safety."
>
> Proverbs 11:14

Wise leaders:

* Seek God's guidance.
* Listen to counsel.
* Remain teachable.
* Learn continuously.
* Make decisions prayerfully.

Leadership is not knowing everything.

Leadership is depending upon God's wisdom while serving others faithfully.

The greatest leaders never stop learning.

---

# Leadership and Humility

Humility is one of the defining characteristics of Kingdom leadership.

Jesus demonstrated humility throughout His earthly ministry.

Paul wrote:

> "Let nothing be done through selfish ambition or conceit, but in lowliness of mind let each esteem others better than himself."
>
> Philippians 2:3

Humility does not mean weakness.

Humility means recognizing dependence upon God.

A humble leader understands:

* Every gift comes from God.
* Every opportunity comes from God.
* Every success belongs ultimately to God.

Humility protects leaders from pride and keeps their focus on God's purposes.

---

# Raising Future Leaders

Biblical leadership is not merely about leading today.

It is about preparing leaders for tomorrow.

Moses mentored Joshua.

Elijah mentored Elisha.

Jesus trained the disciples.

Paul invested in Timothy.

Healthy leadership multiplies itself.

A Kingdom leader asks:

* Who am I developing?
* Who am I mentoring?
* Who will continue this work after me?

The future of any movement depends upon leadership multiplication.

The strongest leaders are those who intentionally raise others.

# The Ultimate Example of Leadership

Jesus Christ remains the perfect model of leadership.

He possessed all authority yet chose service.

He possessed all power yet demonstrated humility.

He led not through domination but through sacrifice.

Mark 10:45 summarizes His example:

> "For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many."

Every Christian leader should look to Christ as the standard.

His life defines leadership.

His character shapes leadership.

His example guides leadership.

Biblical leadership begins with submission to God.

The greatest leaders are not those who seek power but those who faithfully serve God's purposes and people.

Kingdom leadership is built upon:

* Service before status.

Leadership is stewardship.
